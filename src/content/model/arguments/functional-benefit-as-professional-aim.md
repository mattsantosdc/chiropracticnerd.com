## Inferential route

[The actual functional-benefit conclusion](/model/science/actual-chiropractic-benefit/) states that some chiropractic inputs cause neuromotor reorganization that provides a functional benefit within a specified neuromotor scope, context, and timescale. [The argument from actual improvement to benefit](/model/arguments/actual-chiropractic-benefit/) derives that conclusion from [the causal improvement claim](/model/science/chiropractic-inputs/), [the account of reorganization as change](/model/philosophy/adjustment/), and [the value criterion](/model/philosophy/functional-benefit-criterion/). The benefit is the qualifying functional improvement itself.

[The professional-purpose principle](/model/philosophy/benefit-as-professional-aim/) states that a benefit a profession's interventions can produce provides a reason to adopt that benefit as a professional aim. If some chiropractic inputs produce the benefit described in [the actual functional-benefit conclusion](/model/science/actual-chiropractic-benefit/), it is a benefit available through those interventions. Applying [the professional-purpose principle](/model/philosophy/benefit-as-professional-aim/) supplies a reason to adopt facilitating that improvement as a chiropractic aim. This argument treats that reason as supporting the legitimacy asserted in [the functional aim of chiropractic](/model/philosophy/chiropractic-purpose/), subject to relevant objections.

The resulting aim concerns facilitating context-appropriate improvement. An identified neuromotor benefit can supply a rationale for that aim without establishing benefits across every functional domain, for every person, or from every input. [The functional aim of chiropractic](/model/philosophy/chiropractic-purpose/) identifies a legitimate aim, not an exclusive purpose or a guaranteed outcome.

## Why this is defeasible

[The professional-purpose principle](/model/philosophy/benefit-as-professional-aim/) supplies a reason for the aim rather than an unconditional rule that every benefit makes its pursuit legitimate. An ethical objection to adopting an aim, a conflict with appropriate professional responsibilities, or a problem in applying the benefit criterion could defeat this inference while leaving some input-caused benefit intact. The inference is therefore defeasible even if the effect and value premises are granted.

The argument depends on actual scoped benefit as stated in [the actual functional-benefit conclusion](/model/science/actual-chiropractic-benefit/). Intention, a physiological change alone, or the professional aim itself cannot replace that premise. Rejecting the causal improvement claim or its value criterion removes this particular route; it does not establish the aim's falsity or exclude another independently supported argument.

## Functional value and further outcomes

[The functional-benefit criterion](/model/philosophy/functional-benefit-criterion/) attributes value to the functional improvement itself. The argument requires no additional symptom or condition outcome to make that improvement a benefit. Further outcomes, where they occur, can coexist with the functional improvement; they do not supply a missing premise here.

Whether any particular additional outcome is caused through this functional improvement is a separate causal question. This argument does not assume that every symptom change has that route. It also does not depend on open-ended functional potential or on the more specific perturbation mechanism in [the chiropractic perturbation claim](/model/science/chiropractic-perturbation-mechanism/).

Whether to apply an input still requires the person-specific judgment described in [the assessment strategy](/model/art/assessment/). The aim and its rationale do not identify an indication, validate an assessment, or settle the balance of relevant consequences for that person.
