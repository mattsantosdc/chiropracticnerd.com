## A professional purpose

The Model proposes helping people function better in their actual circumstances as a legitimate aim of chiropractic care. **Context-appropriate improvement** means functioning better relative to a stated baseline or alternative, with relevant gains and losses considered together for the functions and period being assessed. The aim guides what chiropractors investigate and try to achieve; it is one legitimate aim, and its justification remains open to examination.

The reasoning brings together the Model's claim that some chiropractic inputs cause functional improvement, its judgment that this improvement is valuable, and an explicit principle that achievable functional benefit can justify a professional aim. The claimed improvement concerns **neuromotor function**: the nervous system's control and coordination of muscle tone, posture and movement. The [benefit argument](/model/arguments/actual-chiropractic-benefit/) establishes the scoped benefit from its premises; the [professional-purpose argument](/model/arguments/functional-benefit-as-professional-aim/) uses that benefit and the professional-purpose principle to support the aim. The causal claim, value judgment and principle remain separately open to examination.

Functional improvement can be valuable whether or not it has an additional consequence such as symptom relief. A person may seek a further outcome while the functional improvement itself remains the aim described here. The causal relationship between that improvement and any further outcome requires its own account.

The Model also describes functional possibilities as open-ended. That informs how possibilities are considered, but the legitimacy of pursuing an achievable functional benefit does not require potential to be open-ended.

Whether pursuing an input is worthwhile additionally depends on the person's goals, costs, risks, burdens, alternatives, and uncertainty. A functional benefit can occur while those considerations weigh against the intervention. The legitimate aim does not remove that judgment or supply a universal score for it.

## Boundary

The rationale concerns the specified neuromotor benefit. It makes no promise of symptom relief or broader health effects, and does not require every symptom change to be mediated by the proposed functional improvement. The Model's definition of a successful adjustment concerns the specified neuromotor comparison; symptoms that affect that comparison remain relevant, even when a separate symptom outcome is not its goal.

The value of better function does not establish that a particular input improves function or justify its application. Whether improvement occurs, whether chiropractic caused it, and whether it produces any additional specific benefit are separate empirical questions. Practical assessment must still address whether proceeding is warranted for this person now.
