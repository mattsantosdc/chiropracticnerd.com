# Model v0.1 reasoning audit

This audit applies the explicit-reasoning method to the substantive spine from P-001 through S-006 and A-003. It evaluates the claims as written on 2026-09-03. Dependency order is not treated as argument order.

## Overall finding

No higher-level scientific conclusion in the current spine is represented as deductively established. That is appropriate. The scientific entries are unresolved causal, mechanistic, measurement, or possibility claims; the Philosophy entries include value choices; and the Art entries are defeasible strategies. The initial argument records make selected methodological and practical routes explicit, and all are classified as defeasible.

No substantive scientific premise was added merely to complete a chain. S-004 and S-005 remain compound at v0.1 because splitting them before operational measures and evidence are ready would create finer labels without greater traceability.

## The S-005/S-006 gap

The current Model correctly does not infer:

1. appropriately selected chiropractic inputs **can** increase neuromotor adaptability (S-005); and
2. changes in nervous-system adaptability **can** influence broader human adaptability (S-006); therefore
3. chiropractic **can** improve broader human adaptability.

The first `can` may be true in one set of people, inputs, measures, conditions, or timescales while the second is true in another. S-005 also does not yet show that its measured neuromotor change is the kind of nervous-system change covered by S-006. The relevant existential scopes therefore need not overlap, so the conclusion does not follow deductively.

### Candidate premises needed for a valid, narrower route

These are identified gaps, not accepted Model entries. They have not been assigned IDs or added to canonical content.

| Candidate premise | Logical role | Content type | Evidence required | Dependent conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Under prespecified conditions C, neuromotor measure N validly indicates the kind of nervous-system adaptability change relevant to broader outcome O. | Connects the S-005 outcome to the construct and outcome pathway addressed by S-006. | Mixed conceptual and empirical measurement-validity claim. | Reliable measurement, construct and discriminant validity, prespecified conditions, and evidence that alternatives such as learning, effort, fatigue, pain, or error do not explain N. | A claim about input I affecting O cannot use N as its bridge without this mapping. |
| Among the same population and conditions C in which input I produces change N, that change causally contributes to a prespecified improvement in O over timescale T. | Forces population, condition, mediator, outcome, and time overlap and supplies the missing transfer step. | Empirical causal/mediation hypothesis. | Prospective controlled studies measuring I, N, and O; temporal ordering; credible comparisons; adequate power; mediation or intervention evidence; durability; replication; and testing of alternative pathways. | Only a narrowed conclusion such as “Under C, input I can improve O through N over T.” |

Even with those premises, deduction would establish only that the narrowed conclusion follows if every premise is true. The empirical truth of S-005 and the bridge premises would remain separately uncertain and evidence-responsive.

## Revision implications

- If S-004's mechanism fails, reconsider S-005's proposed mechanism; do not infer that every possible chiropractic effect is false.
- If S-005 fails, reconsider the intervention-dependent portion of A-001 and the intended mechanism in A-002. S-006 may remain independently open.
- If S-006 is narrowed or rejected, do not automatically reject S-005's neuromotor claim; reject only unsupported downstream routes to broader outcomes.
- A well-powered, valid null result can supply evidence of absence within its tested scope. A weak, mismatched, or insensitive study supplies only an absence of detected evidence.
