## Supported components

The background propositions that nervous systems regulate movement and tone are well established. Experimental work also shows that spinal manipulation can evoke afferent responses and can be followed by changes in selected measures of sensorimotor processing, excitability, motor output, or strength.

## Unproven links

Current evidence does not establish that the model can reliably identify a subluxation, that a selected thrust specifically corrects the identified strategy, that immediate changes represent durable gains in adaptability, or that those gains improve health or flourishing in asymptomatic populations.

Small samples, heterogeneous interventions, comparator problems, indirect measures, short follow-up, and research groups with overlapping authorship further limit inference. v0.1 should be read as a structured research program with plausible components, not a validated unified theory.
