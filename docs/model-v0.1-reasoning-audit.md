# Model v0.1 reasoning audit

This audit evaluates P-001–P-003, S-001–S-006, and A-001–A-003 as written on 2026-09-09, alongside all four structured arguments. Claims and inferential roles were inspected before revising empirical proposals. Dependencies record revision impact, not reasoning or evidence. This revision adds no evidence review or claim of empirical confirmation.

## Overall finding

Open-ended human functional potential is the philosophical starting point. Net-positive functional improvement is the explicit valued aim, not a synonym for change or adaptability. Better function is itself a benefit within this Model, without requiring an additional identified consequence or attribution of that consequence to the improvement. Whether improvement occurred, whether chiropractic caused it, and whether it brings a specific downstream benefit remain separate empirical questions. P-003 permits a rationale without symptoms or pathology; it does not demonstrate effectiveness in asymptomatic people. P-002's boundary now distinguishes a hoped-for symptomatic outcome from the Model's proposed functional action of chiropractic. The focused Philosophy audit below records that clarification and the remaining reasoning and measurement questions.

Science distinguishes a state-dependent neuromotor domain, possible modifiable opportunities, general perturbation, intentional chiropractic use, and possible broader benefit. None silently establishes the next. Art preserves assessment → conditional input → reassessment, with non-intervention and stopping available. The stronger hypothesis that all people may retain chiropractic benefit potential across changing states is compatible with this structure, but is neither required nor established.

All arguments remain defeasible. No scientific conclusion is presented as deductively established, and no empirical premise was created to complete a desired chain. The open assumptions and bridges below limit what may be inferred without blocking an inspectable v0.1.

## Focused Philosophy audit after wording revisions

Scope: review P-001–P-003 as currently written, including removal of the deferred universal-benefit hypothesis sentence from P-003 and the subsequent clarification of P-002's symptom wording. The other canonical claims and arguments are not rewritten by these focused changes. A-003's treatment of new or worsening symptoms remains expressly deferred.

### What holds together

The three main claims provide an inspectable philosophical foundation: human functional potential is open-ended; net-positive functional improvement is a legitimate chiropractic aim; symptoms and pathology are not required to supply that rationale. They do not establish that a particular person needs an adjustment or that a particular input produces improvement.

P-001 is adequately bounded as a framework commitment. Its explanation distinguishes open-ended possibilities across circumstances from unlimited capacity, inevitable progress, or a presently detectable imperfection in everyone. No additional philosophical premise is needed simply to retain this starting point.

P-002 explicitly values improved function itself. A separately identified symptom or health outcome is unnecessary to establish that value. This does not establish that a particular observation represents improvement or that chiropractic caused it. Keeping the entry classified as a value claim preserves that distinction.

P-003's main scope claim is coherent with the stated meanings of P-001 and P-002. ARG-004 exposes the interpretation that an aim legitimate in its own right can supply an independent rationale. Its defeasible classification remains appropriate: the brief premise claims alone do not force every interpretation of their scope, and no efficacy conclusion follows.

### Findings and current status

1. **P-002's symptom wording is clarified.** The boundary now describes the proposed functional action of chiropractic rather than presenting functional and symptomatic purposes as alternative bases for intervention. A person or chiropractor may hope for symptom relief, but that hope changes neither the proposed physiological action nor the functional grounds for indication and selection. Actual net-positive functional improvement remains a successful functional outcome even without the hoped-for symptom change. The claim remains a value claim about a legitimate aim; its boundary previews the Model's empirical account without establishing that account from the value premise. This resolves the wording ambiguity without establishing indicator validity or completing the stronger indication argument below.

2. **The stronger indication principle needs a separate account of its reasoning.** P-003's boundary says symptoms and pathology neither constitute nor contribute to an indication. Non-necessity as a rationale does not itself establish that stronger exclusion. P-001 and P-002, as quoted in ARG-004, therefore do not establish the whole boundary. The intended grounding is the Model's proposed functional action of chiropractic and selection of inputs through indicators of an opportunity for functional improvement, independently of desired symptomatic outcomes. That mechanism-to-indication reasoning is not yet fully exposed in the structured arguments. Calling functional improvement a legitimate aim, or identifying a possible mechanism, does not by itself validate the indicators or their exclusivity as grounds for selection. This is an identified reasoning burden, not a finding that the intended principle is false or merely a discretionary preference. It need not be repaired by inventing P-004 or changing the accepted Philosophy claims now.

3. **“Net-positive” remains an acknowledged evaluative and measurement gap.** P-002 states the aim but supplies no complete criterion for deciding which changes count as better function or how gains and losses are weighed. That is acceptable for the philosophical statement of an aim at v0.1, but not sufficient to certify clinical success. A person's hoped-for outcome must not redefine what an indicator measures; goals and preferences can still inform the value of pursuing care and its burdens. The distinction between measured change, functional improvement, net benefit, and a further symptomatic outcome must survive future operationalization.

### Dependencies, scope, and disposition

The three Philosophy dependencies remain appropriate: P-001 supplies the potential addressed by P-002, and P-003 separately uses P-001's health-status scope and P-002's independent functional aim. Their conceptual and normative roles do not establish a causal effect or deductive proof. Existing IDs, slugs, claim types, and argument references remain valid.

P-003's reference to A-001 is an application cross-reference, not an argument that assessment establishes the Philosophy claim. Its existing direct downstream entry A-001 and the referencing ARG-002 and ARG-004 were inspected. They still describe non-necessity as a rationale; they do not yet fully articulate the stronger functional-indication principle. No reciprocal dependency, new premise, or change to Art is introduced to hide that gap. The removal of the deferred hypothesis sentence changes none of these relationships.

The philosophical foundation can be retained without adding an entry. P-002's wording now distinguishes hoped-for outcomes from the proposed physiological action and functional indication. The substantive future tasks remain making the functional-indication reasoning inspectable and specifying what counts as net-positive functional improvement. The universal benefit-potential hypothesis remains only a deferred question in this audit. Detailed interpretation of post-adjustment symptoms remains outside this focused revision.

## Unresolved reasoning and empirical bridges

These are requirements for stronger claims, not accepted new Model entries.

| Bridge or assumption | What is still needed | Inference currently withheld |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Functional potential → chiropractic benefit | A relevant opportunity, an effective input in the person and state concerned, and net-positive outcomes | Philosophy cannot show that an asymptomatic person, every person, or this person now benefits. |
| State-dependent limitation → modifiable beneficial opportunity | Reliable measures, reference conditions, and evidence that a specified change is possible and beneficial with tradeoffs considered | State dependence, deviation from average, or fewer strategies does not establish S-003 in a particular case. |
| General perturbation → chiropractic instantiation | Prospective salience and input criteria, predicted neural and motor responses, credible comparisons, and tests of alternatives | S-004 does not establish S-005's empirical component. |
| Opportunity + intentional input → actionable match | Detection and predictive selection validity in overlapping people, states, tasks, inputs, and timescales; context and preferences supporting action | S-003's possibility and S-005's intended use do not establish actionability or success. |
| Chiropractic-produced neuromotor change → broader net-positive function | Causal attribution to the input; valid mapping to a mediator or indicator; prespecified broader outcomes; overlapping conditions, adequate duration, controlled comparisons, and net benefit | Immediate response does not confirm S-006 or authorize health or wellness promises. |
| Neural integration → principal chiropractic pathway | Evidence distinguishing neural mediation from competing explanations, with temporal and causal specificity | S-001 does not establish neural mediation or its primacy for chiropractic. |
| Within-cycle response → justified subsequent input | Reliable reassessment, valid decision thresholds, and sufficient expected value for the next input | Neither change nor lack of change makes repeated input obligatory. |

S-006 now **states** the unresolved chiropractic-to-broader-function hypothesis instead of leaving it implicit between generic adaptability claims. This improves inspectability; it supplies no evidence. The former S-005/S-006 wording is no longer the audit's object. Its general lesson survives: possibilities whose cases and conditions need not overlap do not form a deductive causal chain. S-003 and S-005 do not establish a present match, and neither establishes S-006.

Mechanism failure requires revising that explanation, not declaring every possible effect impossible. A broader effect without neural mediation would not validate the proposed pathway. Null findings support absence only within a scope where valid measurement, adequate sensitivity and power, and suitable comparisons could detect the specified effect; weak or mismatched tests establish only failure to detect it.

- **Removed P-001 → S-001 and S-001 → S-002.** Neural integration does not require the philosophical potential commitment; state-dependent neuromotor strategies can be investigated independently of central integration. Shared subject matter is insufficient.

- **Retained P-002, S-003, and S-005 → A-001; added P-003 → A-001.** Assessment uses the aim, possible opportunity, intentional inputs, and scope beyond symptoms. S-006 is not an input-selection gate: A-001 can investigate local neuromotor value without assuming broader transfer.

- **Retained A-001 → A-003 and A-002 → A-003.** Reassessment depends directly on the prediction and the input applied. No A-003 → A-001 same-cycle dependency is added for future feedback.
