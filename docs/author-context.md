## 1. The view of chiropractic to preserve

Matt's positive aim is to help people function and adapt better in their actual circumstances. Functional improvement has value in itself. A person need not first have pain, a disease, or an injury for improvement to matter.

His own practice orientation centers on that functional opportunity. Describe the value being pursued directly: more appropriate control, coordination, and capacity to respond to changing demands. Do not make symptom relief the implicit purpose of every assessment or intervention. Symptoms and pathology remain relevant to the person's circumstances and decisions.

The nervous system is understood as actively organizing the person's behavior. Muscle tone and movement express that organization. A pattern that seems restrictive may have a useful reason, including one the practitioner has not recognized. Finding a different possible strategy does not establish that the current one is defective.

Matt favors an organismic, self-regulating account without metaphysical vitalism. The practitioner supplies an input; the person's nervous system produces the response. His recent metaphor is that the system can “try on” a different strategy, retain it if useful, or abandon it. Use this as an explanatory metaphor, not a claim that every response is beneficial or that the practitioner knows the body's ultimate needs.

The broader project should retain his interest in human function beyond pain and isolated mechanical performance. Define and support particular broader claims separately. A narrow measurement should neither erase the broader question nor stand in as proof of a whole-person benefit.

The current formal purpose statement is deliberately nonexclusive: functional improvement is **a legitimate aim** of chiropractic. Do not silently convert Matt's personal practice orientation into a claim that this is the profession's only legitimate aim. Likewise, a person having further potential does not establish that a particular input is warranted now.

## 2. The neuroadaptive account

Matt's central proposal is that some patterns recognizable in practice reflect the nervous system's current strategy for controlling the body. A strategy can limit available responses in a particular context, and a suitable input can sometimes enable a more useful organization.

Preserve the following explanatory direction:

1. The person's current neural state helps organize descending control, muscle activity, and responsiveness.
2. That organization appears in patterns of tone, posture, movement, and responses to changes in position or demand.
3. Sensory feedback can participate in maintaining or modifying the pattern.
4. A suitably selected input can perturb the organization and provide an occasion for reorganization.
5. Whether the resulting change is useful must be assessed in its context.

This is Matt's proposed synthesis of his practice observations and explanations. It does not require an initial vertebral displacement, local lesion, or abnormal afferent signal to explain every pattern. It also does not eliminate afferent contributions. Initial sensory activity from an input and subsequent feedback shaped by changed motor control are different parts of the proposed process.

Keep the broad account open to multiple levels of neural organization. Matt has explicitly resisted making cortical failure or a single named brainstem pathway mandatory in the foundational explanation. More specific mechanisms belong downstream, where they can be stated and tested separately.

## 3. Subluxation and the move toward opportunity language

Matt is moving away from making “subluxation” the organizing label because it can make a dynamic, context-dependent pattern sound like a discrete thing that is wrong with the person. His current emphasis is the possibility of beneficial change.

He may still use the term with chiropractors as a bridge from familiar practice language. When doing so, explain the intended meaning. Do not import every traditional definition or imply that the account validates all historical uses of the word.

Preserve these distinctions:

| Term | Intended role |
| --- | --- |
| Finding | Something observed or elicited, such as a tone pattern or a change with positioning. |
| Neuromotor constraint | A proposed pattern of control that limits functional options relative to specified demands. |
| Opportunity | The possibility that changing the pattern would improve function in that context. |
| Actionable opportunity | A sufficiently supported judgment that an available input is worth pursuing for this person now. |
| Subluxation | Contextual chiropractic vocabulary for the proposed constraint and opportunity it may present, with its meaning explained. |

An observation is not the underlying construct. A constraint does not automatically offer a beneficial change, and an opportunity does not automatically identify an effective input. Opportunity also does not imply pathology: a person can function adequately and still have a worthwhile possibility for improvement.

“Neuroadaptive finding” has been discussed as a possible bridge term for practitioners. Treat it as available vocabulary, not a finalized replacement requiring a project-wide rename.

The proposed patterns need not be confined to a spinal segment. Matt has described distributed patterns and expression in other motor systems. Distinguish where a pattern is observed from where an input is applied. Do not invent procedures merely because the proposed construct has a broad scope.

## 4. Muscle palpation and observations in practice

Muscle palpation is central to Matt's analysis. It helps him investigate how the neuromotor system is organizing tone and responding to changes in state. The account must be specific enough that a practicing chiropractor can recognize what he examines.

Reported observations that motivate his thinking include:

- Resting tone that is asymmetric or distributed in recurring patterns.
- Tone that persists despite changing from standing to a supported position.
- Local or distant tone changes with head, neck, or joint position.
- Apparent tone that persists or increases when a muscle is passively shortened, while the comparison side becomes substantially less tense.
- Rapid changes in the observed pattern following an input.
- Patterns that become easier or harder to elicit as the person's configuration or state changes.

These are Matt's reported observations in practice, not a validated diagnostic battery. Preserve that distinction without deleting the observations from the explanation.

His question is not merely “Which muscle is tight?” It is closer to: **What pattern is present, under what conditions does it appear, and does that pattern suggest a useful input?** Position, muscle length, relationships among muscles, and changes across conditions matter to the interpretation.

A palpable change is an observation. Inferring altered motor strategy is an interpretation. Assigning the finding to gamma drive, a reticulospinal pathway, or an autonomic state is a further mechanistic claim. Do not collapse those steps or describe palpation as directly measuring the proposed circuitry.

## 5. Gamma drive, reticulospinal systems, and feedback

Gamma drive and reticulospinal control are important parts of Matt's mechanistic thinking and should remain visible in the project context. They should not be silently replaced with a generic claim that chiropractic “affects the nervous system.”

His working account includes these proposals:

- Descending control can bias the sensitivity and responsiveness of the muscle-control system, including through gamma-related regulation of muscle spindles.
- Some persistent or configuration-sensitive tone patterns may reflect a maintained control strategy rather than a fixed local mechanical problem.
- Reticulospinal and related brainstem systems are candidate contributors to distributed patterns and changes in control strategy.
- Altered motor output and proprioceptive feedback can form a reinforcing process. “High-gain proprioceptive feedback” is language he has preferred because it describes a proposed property without automatically labeling it harmful.
- He has explored static gamma bias as a candidate explanation for sustained findings and has questioned whether simple alpha-gamma coactivation or persistent inward currents adequately explain the full pattern.

These are proposed explanations of his findings. Their relative importance, exact circuitry, and identification in practice remain open. In particular, do not treat persistence during passive shortening as a unique signature of gamma drive.

Keep alpha drive, gamma drive, muscle mechanics, spindle signaling, and palpable tone conceptually distinct. The [S-013 discussion](../src/content/model/statements/neural-mediation.md) already does this and cites [Blum and colleagues' spindle mechanics study](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.55177) as background for a candidate feedback route. The study combines passive-stretch recordings in rats with biophysical modeling; independent alpha and gamma contributions were simulated. Preserve those limits rather than treating the reference as evidence that Matt's palpation method identifies gamma activity.

Matt has used “primitive strategies” to describe comparatively constrained or stereotyped organization. Explain the intended functional meaning. Do not turn it into a universal hierarchy in which brainstem control is bad and cortical control is good, or assign every pattern to a fixed pontine-versus-medullary mechanism.

## 6. Patterns and regional strategies

Matt recognizes recurring synergies that he sometimes calls **antigravity/mobilizer** versus **flexor/immobilizer** patterns, with “extensors” and “flexors” as shorthand. His September 2026 description proposes that a region often expresses one dominant strategy rather than both equally. He explicitly calls this an oversimplification of recurring patterns.

Treat these categories as his practice heuristic. Their meaning concerns coordinated function; a muscle's anatomical name alone does not settle its category.

For example, he recently placed coccygeus in his antigravity/mobilizer grouping based on his interpretation of its stabilizing role and contrasting behavior during defecation. This illustrates how he reasons about the categories. The physiological premises and the classification need separate appraisal before becoming formal empirical claims.

He has also explored relationships between mobilizing/extensor patterns and sympathetic activation, and between immobilizing/flexor patterns and parasympathetic or dorsal-vagal accounts. Retain these as exploratory associations. Do not write a one-to-one equivalence among muscle pattern, reticulospinal pathway, autonomic state, and psychological state.

## 7. State dependence and the Neuroadaptive Window

State dependence is central to the Model's practical meaning. A candidate pattern can emerge, disappear, or change as the person's position or demands change. It is therefore inadequate to describe a permanent “subluxation at C2” without the conditions under which the relevant finding occurs.

Matt's **Neuroadaptive Window** is a proposed range of conditions in which the relevant strategy is expressed and a useful response to input is possible. He has also called it a “learning edge.” These are working practice concepts, not established thresholds or validated measurements. Specify candidate conditions and eliciting ranges before observing the response; neither the label nor a changed threshold establishes an actionable opportunity.

Earlier discussions distinguished three situations: flexible control without the candidate pattern, an elicitable pattern potentially open to change, and protective guarding. The practical idea is to attend to when and how the pattern is expressed, rather than treating every form of resistance as the same target. Guarding should not be reclassified as an opportunity merely to keep the intervention indicated.

His historical technique discussions connected setup and direction with the muscle pattern and its eliciting range. Those details belong in a separately specified account of assessment and application methods. Do not infer a universal contact, direction, dose, or safety protocol from this context document.

One prediction he has proposed is that, after a beneficial response, the previous pattern becomes harder to provoke across relevant conditions. That is a candidate prediction to investigate, not the definition of success. A changed threshold alone does not establish better function.

## 8. Input, adjustment, and benefit

Preserve the current canonical distinctions, even where older conversations or ordinary chiropractic language used the words differently:

| Term | Current Model meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Chiropractic input ([S-023](../src/content/model/statements/chiropractic-input-definition.md)) | A deliberately applied neurobiomechanical stimulus intended to influence neuromotor function. |
| Adjustment ([S-024](../src/content/model/statements/adjustment.md)) | Neuromotor reorganization caused by that input: a change in the organization of neuromotor control or coordination. |
| Successful adjustment ([S-025](../src/content/model/statements/successful-adjustment.md)) | An adjustment that improves context-appropriate human function within a specified neuromotor scope, context, and timescale. |

The practitioner delivers the input. The response may or may not occur. An adjustment, under the present definition, can be neutral or harmful; improvement is a separate requirement for success. An uncertain response remains uncertain.

When explaining the work to chiropractors, acknowledge that ordinary usage often calls the delivered procedure an adjustment. Translate that usage explicitly so the reader can follow the argument.

“Perturbation,” “salient information,” and “training stimulus” describe the proposed mechanism or purpose. They do not establish a beneficial response, retained learning, or a reason to intervene. Define salience prospectively rather than inferring that an input was salient solely because a change followed.

## 9. How to represent and evaluate this account

The agent has two connected responsibilities: represent Matt's account accurately and evaluate its support honestly. Neither task substitutes for the other.

Keep these layers visible:

| Layer | Example | Authoring treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value | Appropriate functional improvement matters. | Identify the value judgment. |
| Reported observation | Tone changes with head position. | Preserve the report and its conditions. |
| Interpretation | The finding reflects a state-dependent strategy. | Explain the inference and alternatives. |
| Mechanism | Descending gamma-related control contributes. | State the hypothesis and its evidential status. |
| Prediction in practice | A selected input will improve a specified function. | Specify the predicted outcome before evaluating it. |

Do not weaken every proposition to “may” merely because confidence is unresolved. State what the Model proposes clearly, then represent confidence and evidence separately. Conversely, confidence in an interpretation does not supply evidence for it.

General neuroscience can support plausibility without validating chiropractic assessment, input selection, or benefit. Keep unfavorable, unchanged, and uncertain observations available to the reasoning. Distinguish evidence for a response from evidence for its mechanism, durability, broader consequences, or value.

When a gap appears, identify the smallest missing link. Do not silently fill it with a familiar chiropractic narrative, an assistant-generated theory, or a generic account of manual therapy.

## 10. Writing for practicing chiropractors

Start with a recognizable practice question or practical implication, then let the reader trace the supporting reasoning. The network can support several question-specific entry points without acquiring one compulsory beginning and conclusion.

Prefer concrete descriptions before technical labels: “The tone pattern changes when the head turns” before a proposed tract-level explanation. Introduce technical vocabulary where it helps the reader understand the observation or inference.

During clarity-focused edits, improve the introduction, entry questions, summaries, and opening explanations. Preserve canonical propositions, scope, confidence, and relationships unless a semantic change is explicitly requested. Flag genuine gaps separately.

Use illustrations beside the explanation they clarify, such as showing how the same person can express different findings in different positions. Label hypothetical examples as illustrations. Avoid imagery that quietly reintroduces displaced bones, blocked nerve flow, or measured neural mechanisms that the account has not established.

Use likely objections first as editing questions: What could a reader misunderstand? Which inference is missing? Which word suggests more than intended? Add formal objections when they address a sufficiently clear claim. Follow [objection authoring](objection-authoring.md): an editorial question does not assert a counterpremise, and a formal attacking argument requires an explicit proposition, support, and an exact target. Clarity work should preserve existing evidence boundaries.
