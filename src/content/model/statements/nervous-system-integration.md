## Integration within the whole person

Within [the broader account of active organismic organization](/model/science/organismic-organization/), this statement identifies the nervous system's integrative role in humans: relating information about the person and environment to the organization, coordination, and regulation of function. “Central” identifies an organizing role, not a ranking that places every other biological system beneath it.

This is an additional empirical premise, not a consequence of [the organismic-organization claim](/model/science/organismic-organization/): living organisms do not all have nervous systems. It also stands independently of the philosophical commitment in [the open-ended potential commitment](/model/philosophy/functional-potential/). Open-ended potential is not evidence for neural integration.

## Current evidence

The claim is strongly supported by convergent evidence across three areas:

- **Brain-body and autonomic regulation:** [Sammons and colleagues (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.08.050) review how neural communication links internal bodily state with physiological regulation, behavior, and responses to the environment. Local, reflex, and central processes provide the broadest support for the integrative role asserted here.
- **Sensorimotor integration and coordination:** [Nielsen (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.01073.2003) reviews human experimental evidence that sensory signals from muscles, skin, and joints converge with descending motor commands in spinal circuitry. This directly supports neural integration in coordinated voluntary movement.
- **Interactions with other physiological systems:** [Huh and Veiga-Fernandes (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41577-019-0247-z) review bidirectional communication between neural and immune cells within and across organs. This extends the evidence beyond motor and autonomic output to regulation of tissue and immune physiology.

These reviews synthesize human and animal research; confidence in the broad integrative role does not imply that every specific pathway is established in humans. Confidence is high because independent literatures demonstrate nervous-system integration in motor coordination, internal physiological regulation, behavior, and inter-organ physiology. The claim concerns a central integrative role within an interacting organism, not exclusive neural control of human function.

## Boundary

The person includes interacting endocrine, immune, metabolic, cardiovascular, musculoskeletal, and other systems, as well as an environment and social context. This claim neither makes the nervous system a sole controller nor labels every neural change beneficial.

An integrative role alone does not show that chiropractic influences broader function. [The neural-mediation claim](/model/science/neural-mediation/) separately proposes motor-related neural change as the predominant mediator of broader chiropractic effects and carries the empirical burden of that more specific proposal. General integration provides background for investigating it.
