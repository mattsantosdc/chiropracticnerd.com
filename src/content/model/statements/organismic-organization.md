## Functional organization

**Functional organization** means the coordinated relationships among an organism's parts and processes through which it sustains its activity and responds to its conditions. Maintaining that organization concerns its continuing operation; adapting it concerns changes in those relationships as conditions change. The organism participates through its own biological activity, in interaction with its environment.

“Actively” does not mean conscious choice or an infallible intelligence. The claim concerns biological processes, rather than an inevitable purpose to become healthier or better. It does not require every process to change continuously or maintenance to succeed under every condition. The relevant processes and timescales must be specified for the organism and life stage being investigated.

## Current evidence

**Confidence is high.** Complementary experiments in mammals, bacteria, and plants directly demonstrate biological processes that sustain functioning and modify regulation as conditions change. The studies below were selected for their causal interventions and measured responses, with distinct evidence for maintenance and adaptation.

**Active maintenance and nutrient recycling.** [Komatsu and colleagues (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.200412022) disrupted *Atg7*, a gene required for autophagy, the cellular process that breaks down and recycles internal material. In adult mouse liver, this impaired routine protein turnover, produced abnormal organelles and protein aggregates, and prevented the normal increase in degradation during fasting. The combination of genetic intervention, turnover assays, and microscopy shows that maintaining cellular organization depends on ongoing biological activity and that this activity changes with nutrient availability.

**Adaptation through a biochemical network.** [Alon and colleagues (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1038/16483) measured swimming behavior in *Escherichia coli* before and after sustained exposure to a chemical attractant. Bacteria returned to their previous tumbling frequency despite the continued stimulus. Deleting the gene for the adaptation enzyme CheR disrupted adaptation; restoring its expression restored the response. Varying network protein concentrations changed adaptation time and baseline behavior while adaptation remained precise across the tested range. This demonstrates active recalibration within an organism without a nervous system. Precision of that response is distinct from overall functional improvement.

**Regulation of water loss in plants.** [Mustilli and colleagues (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.007906) identified two independent *Arabidopsis* mutations that disrupted OST1 signaling. Mutant plants failed to close leaf pores normally in response to the drought-related hormone abscisic acid and lost water more rapidly. Other responses, including closure in darkness, remained intact, and introducing the normal gene restored the phenotype. Genetic rescue and these response-specific controls support an active signaling mechanism linking environmental conditions, pore regulation, and water retention.

Together, these findings support the general biological claim by convergence across distinct organisms and mechanisms. Extending that support to living organisms generally is an inductive inference, rather than an exhaustive test of every species and life stage. The maintenance claim concerns processes sustaining organization; the adaptation claim concerns changes in regulation under altered conditions. Neither is established merely by defining something as alive, and neither implies that every response improves the organism's overall functioning.

## Boundary

S-007 separately proposes the nervous system's integrative role in humans. Living organisms do not all have nervous systems, and this general organismic claim does not establish that additional neural premise.

Maintaining or adapting organization need not improve function overall. Responses can preserve one function at a cost to another or fail to meet current demands. The proposition asserts neither universal self-healing nor limitless capacity, inevitable progress, or responsiveness to chiropractic. Its biological content remains empirical even when it serves as a starting point for the Model's philosophical reasoning.
