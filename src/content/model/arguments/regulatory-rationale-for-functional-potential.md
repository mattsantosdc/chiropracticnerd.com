## Inferential route

[The organismic-organization claim](/model/science/organismic-organization/) supplies active maintenance and adaptation of organismic organization. [The neural-integration claim](/model/science/nervous-system-integration/) adds the central integrative role of the nervous system in humans; that additional empirical premise is not entailed by the organismic claim. [The sensory-information account](/model/science/information-in-neural-regulation/) proposes sensory information and experience in estimating relevant conditions. [The regulatory-limits claim](/model/science/regulatory-limits-and-demands/) supplies distinct limits on information, feasible responses, and competing demands. [The state-dependent motor-control claim](/model/science/state-dependent-neuromotor-function/) asserts variation with state, task, and environment, including limited or poorly suited strategies in some circumstances. [The sensory-updating account](/model/science/sensory-updating-and-reorganization/) proposes estimate updating and the capacity for sensory information to contribute to strategy reorganization. None asserts that adaptation must improve function.

Together these premises describe function as maintained and responsive to conditions, with strategies evaluated in context and capacities to change. They motivate treating functional possibilities across changing circumstances as an ongoing question. The interpretive bridge is that a framework for those possibilities should assess function in relation to circumstances and purposes, rather than use health, normality, or symptom status alone as a criterion of completion. This is a revisable principle for framing the question, not an additional empirical finding that improvement is always available.

On that basis, the argument supports [the open-ended potential commitment](/model/philosophy/functional-potential/): “Human functional potential is open-ended.” Its stated meaning is that health and symptom status alone do not identify a final state of completed human function. Its scope concerns possibilities across circumstances, purposes, and stages of life, not unlimited capacity or an improvement available at every moment.

## Why this is defeasible

The exact premises do not entail the exact conclusion. They neither define health status nor rule out a person having no better feasible response under specified conditions. The interpretive bridge makes a framework choice informed by the biological account; it remains open to challenge even if the empirical premises are accepted. Continuing maintenance can occur without any further improvement. A locally best feasible response can also coexist with changing demands and limited information.

The route would require reconsideration if the proposed regulatory account failed, if its scope did not bear on the functional possibilities in question, or if a better framing related health status to completion across the relevant circumstances. Failure of this route would not by itself prove [the open-ended potential commitment](/model/philosophy/functional-potential/) false. Empirical confidence belongs to the premise statements; it does not change [the open-ended potential commitment](/model/philosophy/functional-potential/) into an experimentally established result.

## Opportunities and benefit remain separate

Incomplete information does not show that a better feasible strategy exists. A poorly suited strategy does not show that an accessible alternative exists. Reorganization does not establish beneficial modification. [The neuromotor-opportunity claim](/model/science/neuromotor-opportunity/) separately asserts modifiability that improves function in some cases, using [the contextual comparison](/model/philosophy/context-appropriate-function/). This additional empirical claim supplies no premise for the present rationale: the argument concerns regulatory responsiveness and limits without presupposing beneficial opportunities. Neither [the value criterion](/model/philosophy/functional-benefit-criterion/), [the professional aim](/model/philosophy/chiropractic-purpose/), nor any chiropractic-benefit claim is used to establish the foundation.

General biological adaptability does not establish chiropractic responsiveness. [The proposed opportunities](/model/science/neuromotor-opportunity/) and [the proposed beneficial responses in some cases](/model/science/chiropractic-inputs/) need not coincide in people, states, inputs, or timescales. The argument does not establish that every symptom-free person can benefit now. [The value criterion](/model/philosophy/functional-benefit-criterion/), [the professional aim](/model/philosophy/chiropractic-purpose/), and [the route for a rationale beyond symptoms](/model/arguments/functional-rationale-beyond-symptoms/) from [the open-ended potential commitment](/model/philosophy/functional-potential/), [the functional-benefit criterion](/model/philosophy/functional-benefit-criterion/), and [the functional aim of chiropractic](/model/philosophy/chiropractic-purpose/) to [the rationale beyond symptoms](/model/philosophy/care-beyond-symptoms/) remain distinct from the empirical and practical grounds needed to justify a particular input.
