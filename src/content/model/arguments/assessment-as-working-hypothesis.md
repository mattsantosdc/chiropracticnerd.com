## Inferential route

S-005 supplies the legitimate professional aim of context-appropriate functional improvement. S-006 makes that rationale available without requiring symptoms or pathology. S-009 proposes: “Some motor strategies that limit functional options in a given context can be modified to improve context-appropriate human function within a specified neuromotor scope and timescale.” Its explanation names a candidate pattern a neuromotor constraint and keeps that pattern distinct from the opportunity for beneficial change. S-027 concludes: “Some chiropractic inputs cause neuromotor reorganization that provides a functional benefit within a specified neuromotor scope, context, and timescale.” That mixed empirical and evaluative conclusion depends on S-011 and S-022 through ARG-007; the deduction does not resolve its empirical support.

The practical reasoning scheme is conditional: when pursuing a legitimate aim through an uncertain intervention, identify the relevant present opportunity, assess whether any available input has sufficient expected value, and state the prediction as a revisable working hypothesis. This decision principle is the argument's normative bridge; it is not a causal fact derived from S-009 or S-027. It motivates S-014's assessment strategy and its “what, if any, input” condition, without requiring action.

The scheme also uses the Model's proposed target of input selection: its target is a neuromotor constraint that an available input could modify beneficially. A symptom or diagnosis is not itself that target and does not establish the required opportunity. If the opportunity is absent, the proposed input cannot accomplish that neuromotor improvement. Safety and comfort are additional considerations: symptoms or pathology may warrant withholding or modifying an input without supplying its functional indication. This makes the practical target requirement explicit; it is not deduced from S-006's non-necessity claim or offered as evidence that the proposed mechanism is correct.

## Scope and unresolved premises

Even assuming the empirical commitments are true, S-009's modifiable opportunities and S-027's beneficial chiropractic effects need not coincide in the same person, state, task, input, or timescale. S-009 does not establish that an opportunity is detectable or responsive to chiropractic. S-027 asserts functional benefit in some cases; it supplies neither a validated rule for choosing an input nor a guarantee of success in the present case. S-006 permits a rationale without symptoms; it supplies no efficacy evidence in asymptomatic people.

A warranted decision to proceed would additionally require sufficiently reliable observations, a credible match between the proposed opportunity and available input, and a benefit-risk judgment that accounts for the person's goals, preferences, safety, alternatives, and context. Those person-level conditions remain to be established; they are not accepted empirical premises hidden in this argument. An observed neuromotor response would also be insufficient to establish broader benefit. Even established functional benefit can fail to justify the costs, risks, or burdens of an intervention compared with alternatives. The conclusion concerns a prospective practical judgment, not retrospective identification of successful adjustments.

The distinction between symptoms and input targets does not establish that every symptom or diagnosis is empirically unrelated to neuromotor function. Nor does failure to detect a target establish that none exists. The validity of particular indicators and the proposed mechanism require independent evaluation. An input without reorganization is not an adjustment under S-024, but it remains a delivered input relevant to evaluating the assessment, selection, and mechanism. Reorganization without benefit and adverse responses also remain in the accounting. Uncertain findings cannot be turned into success or failure by definition.

The conclusion is therefore a defeasible strategy for inquiry and selection, not proof that the assessment works or that an actionable opportunity exists. No actionable opportunity identified is an admissible result. A candidate constraint specifies what assessment seeks to evaluate; its definition supplies no evidence for detection, beneficial change, or input selection.
