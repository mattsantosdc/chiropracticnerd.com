## Motor strategies and current demands

**Neuromotor function** encompasses the neural regulation of muscle tone, posture, and movement in interaction with the body and environment. A **motor strategy** is a pattern of muscle activation and coordination that supports posture or task performance. Its expression depends on the person's current state and the demands of the task and environment.

Assessment in this domain may consider resting muscle tone, postural support, and responses to changes in position or load. The relevant question is whether the available motor strategies match current demands. A strategy can work well in one context and be poorly matched to another without indicating pathology. A restricted range of strategies or a difference from average does not by itself establish a limitation worth changing.

## Current evidence

Human experiments provide complementary support for the claim:

- **State and context:** [Carpenter and colleagues (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002210100681) studied eight healthy young adults standing under different levels of postural threat. Greater threat produced increased postural stiffness and changes in muscle activity and sway. Motor control changed with the person's circumstances without requiring a pathological disorder.
- **Task requirements:** [Gribble and colleagues (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.01020.2002) varied pointing-target size while controlling movement speed in 16 adults. Smaller targets elicited greater cocontraction, reduced trajectory variability, and improved endpoint accuracy. Greater opposing-muscle activation could therefore serve the task's accuracy demands rather than indicate dysfunction.
- **Environmental dynamics and mismatch:** [Shadmehr and Mussa-Ivaldi (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-05-03208.1994) introduced robot-generated force fields during reaching. Previously effective control initially produced large trajectory errors under the new dynamics. Practice restored trajectories toward their earlier form, and removing the field produced aftereffects. This supports a mismatch between expressed control and changed demands, followed by adaptation; it does not map every strategy the person could potentially use.

**Confidence is high.** These experiments demonstrate that motor control changes with a person's state, task requirements, and environmental dynamics, including circumstances in which current control is poorly matched to changed demands and subsequently adapts. They support context-dependent neuromotor control without establishing that any particular observed pattern is dysfunctional or a target for chiropractic care. Identifying such a pattern reliably and showing that a chiropractic input can modify it beneficially require separate evidence.

## Boundary

“In some circumstances” specifies where the Model proposes limited or poorly matched strategies occur. It does not assert that every strategy or person is limited. Confidence in the proposition is assessed separately. Increased tone, stiffness, asymmetry, or any particular strategy is not inherently dysfunctional; its fit must be judged against the actual demands.

Interpretation of muscle tone, posture, or movement must account for neural activity, tissue properties, learning, effort, fatigue, pain, expectations, and measurement error. A finding alone does not identify its cause or establish a particular motor strategy. Assessment methods require reliability, construct validity, and appropriate reference conditions; v0.1 does not endorse a particular test.

[The contextual improvement criterion](/model/philosophy/context-appropriate-function/) specifies how to compare fit to actual demands, including relevant functional gains and losses. State-dependent variation does not itself establish improvement, and lower tone or greater symmetry is not a universal criterion.

This claim is investigated independently of [the integrative role](/model/science/nervous-system-integration/). State dependence or limitation alone establishes neither modifiability nor benefit. [The neuromotor-opportunity claim](/model/science/neuromotor-opportunity/) takes on that additional empirical question.
