## Inferential route

[The assessment strategy](/model/art/assessment/) states: “Chiropractic assessment develops a working hypothesis about whether a presently actionable opportunity for beneficial neuromotor change can be identified and what, if any, available input is most likely to produce that change.”

[The hypothesis-guided delivery principle](/model/philosophy/hypothesis-guided-application/) states: “When an intervention is sufficiently justified, its delivery should pursue the benefit identified in the assessment and preserve a clear prediction against which the response can be evaluated.”

Together these support [the application strategy](/model/art/chiropractic-application/): “When proceeding is warranted, a selected chiropractic input is applied as a neurobiomechanical training stimulus intended to test and act on the working hypothesis by beneficially altering the targeted neuromotor pattern.”

[The assessment strategy](/model/art/assessment/) identifies the assessment's proposed target, input and beneficial response. [The hypothesis-guided delivery principle](/model/philosophy/hypothesis-guided-application/) supplies the practical reason for keeping delivery aligned with that assessment and its prediction. Applied to the chiropractic input and intended neuromotor change described in [the assessment strategy](/model/art/assessment/), this supports [the conditional application strategy](/model/art/chiropractic-application/). “Training stimulus” names the intended role of that input; it supplies no premise that learning, retention or improvement occurs.

## The condition for proceeding remains open

The conclusion is a strategy for what to do when proceeding is warranted. Neither premise asserts that this condition is satisfied in any particular case. An assessment can identify no actionable opportunity, or its findings and input selection can remain insufficiently supported. A plausible mechanism or an opportunity to learn cannot replace the required justification.

The same person, target, proposed input, functional scope, context and timescale must connect the assessment, delivery and prediction. The person's priorities, choice, alternatives and relevant consequences remain part of the decision. Changed circumstances require reconsideration before delivery. The argument selects no technique, dose or threshold, and it does not claim that the available observations can reliably identify benefit.

## Why this is defeasible

[The hypothesis-guided delivery principle](/model/philosophy/hypothesis-guided-application/) gives a practical reason for assessment-guided, evaluable delivery. A conflict with the person's interests, an uninformative prediction, or a reason to change or withhold the selected input can defeat this application. Adopting a working assessment does not logically necessitate this particular strategy. The normative bridge and its use can be challenged separately from the empirical effect claims.

[The application strategy](/model/art/chiropractic-application/) is supported through this argument and is not also independently assumed. Withdrawing [the hypothesis-guided delivery principle](/model/philosophy/hypothesis-guided-application/) or defeating this inference removes this route to the application strategy and [the reassessment argument](/model/arguments/reassessment-after-application/) that uses it. That loss of support does not assert that either strategy is false.

## Mechanism guidance

[The perturbation account](/model/science/chiropractic-perturbation-mechanism/) can inform the intended disturbance and predicted response. It is not a premise of this argument. Its claim about some improving responses does not identify the warranted input for a particular person. Revising that mechanism requires reconsidering delivery choices and predictions that rely on it; independently justified effects can still support application with an appropriately revised explanation.

[The reassessment strategy](/model/art/reassessment-cycle/) addresses examining the subsequent findings. A response consistent with the prediction can inform the hypothesis without proving causation, the perturbation mechanism or a need for further input.
