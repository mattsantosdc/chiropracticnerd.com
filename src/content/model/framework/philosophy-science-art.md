## Why this is the framework

Philosophy, Science, and Art are connected jobs within one revisable Model, not independent departments that may generate incompatible versions of chiropractic.

**Philosophy** clarifies the questions being asked, defines concepts, exposes assumptions, and identifies values and purposes. It constructs proposed explanations and models and makes the premises and logical relationships behind conclusions explicit. Philosophy can show what a proposal means and whether its reasoning is coherent; it does not determine whether an empirical premise is true.

**Science** tests empirical premises, mechanisms, predictions, and consequences against observation. Evidence constrains what the Model may claim about reality and raises or lowers confidence in its empirical premises. When warranted, findings must narrow, replace, or reject premises, mechanisms, explanations, and downstream conclusions.

**Art** applies the currently best-supported philosophical and scientific understanding to a particular person and context. Practice can generate observations, reveal limitations, and send better questions upstream. Apparent usefulness in practice is not a substitute for evidence and does not by itself establish an explanation or causal effect.

## Epistemic orientation

**Guided by first principles. Tested against reality.**

First principles help make the Model's concepts, assumptions, values, and proposed structure visible and coherent. They do not allow chiropractic to be deduced directly from abstract principles of nature. Every claim about what exists, how it works, or what effects it produces remains answerable to evidence.

The flow is iterative: philosophy makes the proposal inspectable, science tests its empirical burden, art applies the best-supported understanding, and evidence or observations may require the proposal and its consequences to be revised.

## Historical continuity and boundary

Chiropractors already recognize the profession as a philosophy, science, and art. This entry keeps that inherited structure while assigning each part a disciplined role. The ordering is a working methodological commitment, not a claim that the historical profession always used these words consistently or that practical learning flows in only one direction.
