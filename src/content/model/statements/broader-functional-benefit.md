## Broader functional gains

S-012 proposes a causal contribution from chiropractic-induced changes in motor-related neural circuitry to broader functional improvement. The neural processes can participate in both motor and other functions, or influence connected circuits through changed synaptic input. Proprioceptive feedback can contribute without being required for every central interaction. S-030 states the general circuit-influence capacity; S-031 separately predicts a likelihood of broader neural effects.

An observed motor improvement need not itself cause the broader improvement. Motor and other outcomes can be expressions of a shared neural change, or consequences of influence between circuits. A motor measure can indicate part of that change without being its mediator or uniquely identifying the underlying state. Neither every motor-related neuron nor every neural change must have a measurable non-motor consequence.

Local neuromotor improvement is already valuable under S-022. This statement proposes additional improvement, judged using S-021's comparison of relevant gains and losses within specified broader functions, context, baseline or alternative, and timescale. The initial scope must be identified before observing results; it cannot be narrowed retrospectively to make a finding count as broader transfer.

Studies must establish the input's causal contribution to the relevant neural change and that change's contribution to a prespecified broader improvement, preserving population, state, input, and timescale overlap. Shared-process and circuit-influence explanations require evidence appropriate to their respective causal claims. A favorable before-and-after association does not distinguish them or exclude another explanation. Consequences outside the broader functional comparison additionally bear on whether an intervention is worthwhile.

S-013 separately proposes neural mediation as the principal route to broader chiropractic effects. This statement concerns broader improvement, valued as benefit under S-022; it does not require that neural mediation be the principal pathway. Neither hypothesis establishes the other.

## Current evidence

This statement has not yet documented evidence establishing the proposed broader beneficial contribution. The network and anatomical studies discussed in S-030 and S-031 inform possible mechanisms and outcome selection; they do not establish chiropractic-induced broader improvement. Even assuming S-011's local improvement and S-031's likelihood of broader neural effects, qualifying broader improvement requires an additional empirical bridge.

## Boundary

“Some” asserts occurrence in some cases without assigning its frequency. It is distinct from S-031's proposed likelihood of broader processing changes. This benefit hypothesis remains an explicit empirical starting premise in the working theory. No argument derives it from connectivity, local improvement, or likely spread alone. A future route must supply evidence-backed or explicitly adopted, independently testable bridges for the same causal cases and qualifying broader outcomes.

Failure of a specified transfer hypothesis requires revising that hypothesis; it does not establish the impossibility of every chiropractic effect by another route. Specific testable predictions and evidence remain necessary.
