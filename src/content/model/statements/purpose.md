## A professional purpose

A model of chiropractic needs an explicit aim to guide its scientific questions and practical judgments. This statement adopts a professional-purpose commitment: facilitating context-appropriate functional improvement is a legitimate aim in its own right, without making it chiropractic's exclusive aim. S-004 frames the possibilities addressed by this aim as open-ended; it does not establish that improvement is available in a particular case.

S-021 defines improvement as a contextual comparison that considers relevant functional gains and losses together. S-022 separately adopts the value of such improvement. Its value does not depend on identifying an additional consequence, such as symptom relief or a particular health outcome. That value informs the professional aim; it does not by itself establish the legitimacy of every means of pursuing it.

Whether pursuing an input is worthwhile additionally depends on the person's goals, costs, risks, burdens, alternatives, and uncertainty. A functional benefit can occur while those considerations weigh against the intervention. The legitimate aim does not remove that judgment or supply a universal score for it.

## Boundary

The Model's actual neuromotor improvement claim is S-011, and ARG-007 combines it with S-022 to conclude functional benefit in S-027. Those claims do not promise symptom relief or broader health effects. A hoped-for symptomatic outcome does not replace the functional basis for selecting an input. Success in S-025 concerns the specified neuromotor improvement; symptoms that affect that comparison remain relevant, even when a separate symptom outcome is not its goal.

The value of better function does not establish that a particular input improves function or justify its application. Whether improvement occurs, whether chiropractic caused it, and whether it produces any additional specific benefit are separate empirical questions. Art must still address whether proceeding is warranted for this person now.
