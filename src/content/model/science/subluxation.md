## Why this claim is present

The Model needs a concise account of what “subluxation” means before asking whether it can be observed or changed. This definition locates the concept in neuromotor adaptability and treats its expression as dependent on the person's present state and demands.

“Pattern” indicates that subluxation is not established by one isolated finding. “Constrained” indicates fewer or less context-appropriate response options, not merely difference from an average. “State-dependent” allows the pattern to change across tasks, environments, and internal conditions rather than behaving like a timeless lesion.

## Boundary

A definition does not prove that subluxation exists as a distinct phenomenon, occurs frequently, causes harm, can be located reliably, or responds to chiropractic input. The terms state, pattern, constraint, and relevant reference conditions all require later operational definitions and empirical testing.
