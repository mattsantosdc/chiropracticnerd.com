## A professional purpose

A model of chiropractic needs an explicit aim to guide its scientific questions and practical judgments. ARG-008 supports facilitating context-appropriate functional improvement as a legitimate aim in its own right. It combines S-027's account of actual scoped functional benefit with S-029's principle connecting achievable benefit to professional purpose. The aim is nonexclusive and its justification remains open to examination.

S-021 defines improvement as a contextual comparison that considers relevant functional gains and losses together. S-022 values that improvement itself. S-011 asserts that some inputs cause neuromotor reorganization that realizes it, and ARG-007 derives the corresponding functional benefit in S-027. ARG-008 uses that benefit to support the professional aim. The causal claim, value criterion and professional-purpose principle each have a distinct role.

Functional improvement can be valuable whether or not it has an additional consequence such as symptom relief. A person may seek a further outcome while the functional improvement itself remains the aim described here. The causal relationship between that improvement and any further outcome requires its own account.

S-004 frames functional possibilities as open-ended. It informs how possibilities are considered, but the legitimacy of pursuing an achievable functional benefit does not require potential to be open-ended.

Whether pursuing an input is worthwhile additionally depends on the person's goals, costs, risks, burdens, alternatives, and uncertainty. A functional benefit can occur while those considerations weigh against the intervention. The legitimate aim does not remove that judgment or supply a universal score for it.

## Boundary

The rationale concerns the specified neuromotor benefit. It makes no promise of symptom relief or broader health effects, and does not require every symptom change to be mediated by the proposed functional improvement. Success in S-025 concerns the specified neuromotor comparison; symptoms that affect that comparison remain relevant, even when a separate symptom outcome is not its goal.

The value of better function does not establish that a particular input improves function or justify its application. Whether improvement occurs, whether chiropractic caused it, and whether it produces any additional specific benefit are separate empirical questions. Art must still address whether proceeding is warranted for this person now.
