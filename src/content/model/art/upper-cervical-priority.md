## Current rationale

The upper cervical region has unusually rich proprioceptive relationships and close integration with head orientation, vestibular information, postural control, and descending motor organization. The working art therefore checks whether changing an upper-cervical pattern reorganizes findings elsewhere before adding more inputs.

## What is not claimed

This is not a rule that every person has an upper-cervical subluxation, that the upper cervical spine is always primary, or that no other region should be addressed first. The assessment, safety context, and observed response remain controlling.
