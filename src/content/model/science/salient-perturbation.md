## Why this claim is present

This is the Model's independent empirical mechanism hypothesis. It proposes that a neurobiomechanical change which is sufficiently salient to the nervous system can disturb an established motor strategy, prompting the system to reevaluate and reorganize its response. Its dependency on S-002 supplies the meaning of neuromotor adaptability, not evidence for the mechanism.

S-004 remains an empirical root: v0.1 does not yet state another core claim that supplies empirical support for this mechanism. Its conceptual dependency does not resolve that evidential responsibility.

## Boundary

The modal language is deliberate. A perturbation can prompt reorganization; it need not do so. Reorganization can potentially increase adaptability; it may instead be neutral, transient, or unhelpful. The claim does not specify the necessary salience, dose, timing, person, motor strategy, outcome measure, or duration, and it does not establish that a particular chiropractic input meets those conditions.
