## Inferential route

S-027 states that some chiropractic inputs cause neuromotor reorganization that provides a functional benefit within a specified neuromotor scope, context, and timescale. ARG-007 derives that conclusion from S-011's causal improvement claim, S-024's account of reorganization as change, and S-022's value criterion. The benefit is the qualifying functional improvement itself.

S-029 states that a benefit a profession's interventions can produce provides a reason to adopt that benefit as a professional aim. If some chiropractic inputs produce the benefit described in S-027, it is a benefit available through those interventions. Applying S-029 supplies a reason to adopt facilitating that improvement as a chiropractic aim. This argument treats that reason as supporting the legitimacy asserted in S-005, subject to relevant objections.

The resulting aim concerns facilitating context-appropriate improvement. An identified neuromotor benefit can supply a rationale for that aim without establishing benefits across every functional domain, for every person, or from every input. S-005 identifies a legitimate aim, not an exclusive purpose or a guaranteed outcome.

## Why this is defeasible

S-029 supplies a reason for the aim rather than an unconditional rule that every benefit makes its pursuit legitimate. An ethical objection to adopting an aim, a conflict with appropriate professional responsibilities, or a problem in applying the benefit criterion could defeat this inference while leaving some input-caused benefit intact. The inference is therefore defeasible even if the effect and value premises are granted.

The argument depends on actual scoped benefit as stated in S-027. Intention, a physiological change alone, or the professional aim itself cannot replace that premise. Rejecting the causal improvement claim or its value criterion removes this particular route; it does not establish the aim's falsity or exclude another independently supported argument.

## Functional value and further outcomes

S-022 attributes value to the functional improvement itself. The argument requires no additional symptom or condition outcome to make that improvement a benefit. Further outcomes, where they occur, can coexist with the functional improvement; they do not supply a missing premise here.

Whether any particular additional outcome is caused through this functional improvement is a separate causal question. This argument does not assume that every symptom change has that route. It also does not depend on open-ended functional potential or on the more specific perturbation mechanism in S-028.

Whether to apply an input still requires the person-specific judgment described in S-014. The aim and its rationale do not identify an indication, validate an assessment, or settle the balance of relevant consequences for that person.
