## Broader functional gains

M-012 proposes a downstream causal relationship: some neuromotor changes produced by chiropractic contribute to functional gains beyond the immediate neuromotor response. Improved neuromotor function can itself be valuable under M-005; this entry proposes additional, broader gains. Whether that proposition is true remains an empirical question. “Net-positive” requires judging meaningful gains together with relevant harms, burdens, and tradeoffs, using M-005's criterion. A change in a test is not, by itself, broader health, wellness, or functional benefit.

Studies must establish the input's causal contribution to the neuromotor change, the contribution of that change to a prespecified broader outcome, and relevant population, state, dose, and timescale overlap. The value of any broader gain must be assessed over an appropriate duration and weighed against harms, burdens, and tradeoffs. A neuromotor measure may be an indicator rather than a mediator of change; association alone cannot establish its causal role.

M-013 separately proposes neural mediation as the principal route to broader chiropractic effects. This entry concerns net-positive benefit; it does not require that neural mediation be the principal pathway. Neither hypothesis establishes the other.

## Current evidence

This entry has not yet documented evidence establishing the proposed broader beneficial transfer. A neuromotor response alone does not establish that transfer. Even if M-011's beneficial neuromotor-effect hypothesis is true, broader benefit requires separate evaluation.

## Boundary

“Some” specifies that the proposed transfer occurs in some cases, without a frequency estimate or a universal promise. This is the Model's hypothesis; its empirical support remains unresolved. Immediate change does not establish causation, durability, mechanism, or broader benefit. Evidence that an input changes a neuromotor measure must not stand in for evidence of transfer.

Failure of a specified transfer hypothesis requires revising that hypothesis; it does not establish the impossibility of every chiropractic effect by another route. Specific testable predictions and evidence remain necessary.
