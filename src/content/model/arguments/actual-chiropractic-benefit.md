## Exact route

[The functional-benefit criterion](/model/philosophy/functional-benefit-criterion/) states: “Any change that improves context-appropriate human function within a specified functional scope, context, and timescale provides a functional benefit within that scope, context, and timescale.”

[The adjustment definition](/model/philosophy/adjustment/) states: “An adjustment is neuromotor reorganization caused by a chiropractic input, where reorganization means a change in the organization of neuromotor control or coordination.”

[The beneficial neuromotor-effect claim](/model/science/chiropractic-inputs/) states: “Some chiropractic inputs cause neuromotor reorganization that improves context-appropriate human function within a specified neuromotor scope, context, and timescale.”

Therefore [the actual functional-benefit conclusion](/model/science/actual-chiropractic-benefit/): “Some chiropractic inputs cause neuromotor reorganization that provides a functional benefit within a specified neuromotor scope, context, and timescale.”

Assume [the beneficial neuromotor-effect claim](/model/science/chiropractic-inputs/) true and take one of its input-produced reorganizations. [The adjustment definition](/model/philosophy/adjustment/) defines reorganization as a change in neuromotor control or coordination. That very change improves function under [the beneficial neuromotor-effect claim](/model/science/chiropractic-inputs/). [The functional-benefit criterion](/model/philosophy/functional-benefit-criterion/) attributes functional benefit to it within the same scope, context, and timescale. The input causation and existential quantifier are retained; the conclusion adds neither broader transfer nor a new causal link. A neuromotor scope is a functional scope, so the value premise applies without changing domains of outcome.

## What remains empirical

[The beneficial neuromotor-effect claim](/model/science/chiropractic-inputs/) must be true for this route to establish actual benefit. Its unresolved confidence is not improved by the deduction. [The general modifiability claim](/model/science/neuromotor-opportunity/) and [the sensory-updating capacity](/model/science/sensory-updating-and-reorganization/) cannot replace the joint causal premise: their cases need not overlap with chiropractic inputs that produce improving reorganization.

The success definition and [the conclusion about successful adjustments and benefit](/model/philosophy/success-and-functional-benefit/) are unnecessary premises here. Defining success supplies no occurrence evidence. Likewise, [the perturbation explanation](/model/science/chiropractic-perturbation-mechanism/) is a separate empirical commitment; the benefit inference can remain valid if another mechanism explains [the proposed improving effect](/model/science/chiropractic-inputs/).

[The capacity for neural influence](/model/science/neural-network-influence/) and [the predicted broader neural effects](/model/science/broader-neural-effects/) are not premises of this scoped benefit argument. [The broader functional-benefit claim](/model/science/broader-functional-benefit/) separately proposes broader improvement. A change in shared neural processes or influence between circuits could explain that outcome without the observed local motor improvement being its cause; this argument supplies no such causal or outcome bridge.

None of the premises supplies a success frequency, prospective selection rule, reliable assessment, durable change beyond the specified interval, broader benefit, principal neural mediation, or sufficient justification to proceed with an intervention. [The biological rationale for open-ended potential](/model/arguments/regulatory-rationale-for-functional-potential/) and the practical arguments remain defeasible. The conclusion preserves [the functional-benefit scope](/model/philosophy/functional-benefit-criterion/) throughout; it makes no judgment about the intervention's worth after all costs, risks, and alternatives are considered.
