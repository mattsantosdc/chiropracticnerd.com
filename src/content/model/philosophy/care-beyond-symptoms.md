## Why this claim is present

P-001 declines to treat normality or good health as an endpoint to functional potential. P-002 adopts net-positive functional improvement as a legitimate aim in its own right. On that basis, the Model does not require symptoms, injury, disease, or identifiable pathology to supply the rationale for considering chiropractic care. The structured route and its limits are recorded in ARG-004.

## Boundary

A functional rationale for chiropractic care does not, by itself, establish that a particular input will improve function or that proceeding is justified for a particular person.

Within this Model, an adjustment is directed at beneficially modifying a neuromotor constraint. Its indication rests on a working assessment of that opportunity, as described in A-001. Symptoms, injury, disease, and identifiable pathology are not themselves indicators for an adjustment: their presence does not establish a neuromotor constraint or an opportunity to improve function by modifying it.

Symptoms and pathology may nevertheless matter to the assessment of safety and comfort. They may require withholding an adjustment or modifying how it is delivered. Considering them for those purposes does not supply a missing adjustment target, and a person's hoped-for symptomatic outcome does not change the functional basis for selecting the input.

If a person has pain or a medical problem but no relevant neuromotor constraint, there is no target for an adjustment under this account. Even when a constraint is present, it must offer an opportunity for beneficial change through an available input. Without that opportunity, an adjustment cannot produce the proposed neuromotor improvement at that time. This is a conditional consequence of the Model's proposed target and action; identifying whether such an opportunity exists remains an empirical and assessment question.

Nothing here establishes that everyone always needs or can benefit from an adjustment.
