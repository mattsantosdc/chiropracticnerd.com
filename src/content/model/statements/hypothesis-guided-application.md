## Acting on a justified assessment

This practical principle connects a justified decision with how an intervention is delivered. It gives a reason to pursue the benefit for which the intervention was selected and retain a prospective prediction that can be compared with subsequent findings. It applies beyond any particular proposed mechanism.

The condition must be met before proceeding. The desire to test an idea, an interesting mechanism, or the possibility of learning from a response does not itself establish sufficient justification. That judgment must account for the person's goals and preferences, relevant consequences, alternatives, and uncertainty. If circumstances change or the person declines, the earlier judgment does not require delivery.

## Prediction and evaluation

The prediction identifies the intended change, its functional scope, relevant context and timescale, and observations that would count for or against it. Specifying those features makes the intended application inspectable; it does not validate the measurements or guarantee an informative response. An uncertain observation must remain uncertain, and an unfavorable result must not be rescued by changing the original prediction afterward.

ARG-010 uses this principle with S-014's assessment strategy to support S-015's conditional application strategy. The reason concerns fidelity to the assessment and the ability to evaluate what happens. Neither a particular delivery method nor its effectiveness follows from this principle.

## Boundary

This is an explicitly adopted normative commitment, not an empirical discovery or an inviolable rule. Its application remains open to practical objections. Preserving a test must not override a reason to change or withhold the input. Comparing a response with a prediction can inform a working hypothesis without establishing that the intervention caused the response, that a proposed mechanism occurred, or that another input is warranted.
