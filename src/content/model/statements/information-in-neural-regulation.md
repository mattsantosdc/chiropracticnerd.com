## Information for regulation

Sensory information includes relevant signals from within the body and from its surroundings. Prior experience includes effects of earlier interaction and learning; it need not be conscious recollection. An estimate is a working representation of relevant conditions, not necessarily a conscious judgment or an explicit numerical calculation.

This proposition describes a way neural regulation operates within S-007's interacting whole person. It does not make sensory estimation the whole of human regulation, require one comprehensive internal model, or claim that every neural process uses the same computation. The information-and-experience account is an additional empirical commitment, not a deduction from the nervous system having an integrative role.

## Current evidence

Körding and Wolpert studied reaching with displaced visual feedback. Participants' corrections reflected both the learned distribution of displacements and feedback reliability. The reported pattern favored Bayesian integration over the two tested alternatives: compensation from current feedback alone and a learned mapping insensitive to feedback uncertainty. The authors distinguished qualitative agreement from a quantitative demonstration of optimality. [Read the paper](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~kazdan/proof/notes/Bayes/Bayes-predict1_files/DynaPage_002.html).

This is behavioral evidence from a particular task. It does not directly identify neural implementation, exclude every alternative computation, or establish the proposition across human regulation. Confidence in that broader scope remains unresolved; a comprehensive appraisal of internal and external sensing, prior experience, and function-specific mechanisms is still needed.

## Boundary

“Available” does not mean complete, accurate, or objectively best. The proposal does not define better function as globally more accurate prediction or require universal predictive processing. An estimate's contribution to regulation and the value of the resulting response are separate questions.
