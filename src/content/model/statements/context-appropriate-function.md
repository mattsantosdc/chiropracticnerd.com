## Suited to actual conditions

The Model uses **context-appropriate function** to describe how functional organization fits actual conditions. Functional organization concerns the relationships among processes that sustain activity and respond to conditions. In the neuromotor domain, the comparison concerns control and coordination of muscle tone, posture, and movement. Suitability concerns meeting identified demands with the person's available capacities; it is not defined as whatever is beneficial.

The person's goals help identify relevant tasks and priorities. Practical demands, biological limits, and competing functions also matter: a preferred movement cannot be assessed apart from the support, coordination, or effort it requires. Different defensible priorities can produce different judgments. Neither a clinician nor the person needs complete knowledge of the organism or access to a uniquely optimal strategy to make a bounded comparison.

## What an improvement claim must specify

Name the functions being considered, the person's internal state and environment, the relevant demands, the baseline or alternative, and the timescale. Explain how the proposed change better serves those demands and what relevant functions it compromises. The functional scope can be local, such as coordination during an identified task; that does not make it a claim about every aspect of the person's functioning.

A gain in one measurement can coexist with losses in stability, accuracy, endurance, comfort, or another relevant function. Those losses must enter the comparison when they bear on its stated scope. If competing gains and losses cannot be resolved sufficiently to judge improved fit, the improvement remains uncertain. Narrowing the reported scope can describe a particular gain, but must not conceal known losses elsewhere or turn the narrower finding into a claim of broader improvement. Criteria should be specified before observing an intervention's result and revisions disclosed.

Better function is not equated with a globally more accurate internal model, one ideal posture, lower muscle tone, greater symmetry, or a favorable change in any single test. A measurement needs an independently justified relationship to the functions and demands being assessed. The Model supplies no universal score, validated measurement method, or fixed duration threshold.

## Improvement and the decision to intervene

This definition identifies the functional comparison. S-022 separately adopts its value. Neither definition nor value establishes that an intervention produces improvement.

A functional gain can occur while treatment costs, risks, burdens, alternatives, or harms outside the reported scope make pursuing the intervention unwarranted. Functional losses and burdens affecting the specified functions enter the improvement comparison; the decision about an intervention additionally considers its other consequences and uncertainty. A bounded functional benefit is not a judgment that the intervention is worthwhile when all relevant considerations are included.
