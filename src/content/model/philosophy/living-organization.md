## Why it matters

Living things do not merely occupy an organized state. They spend energy maintaining boundaries, coordinating parts, responding to disturbances, repairing damage, learning, developing, and changing their behavior. The organization is dynamic and embodied.

This provides a modern bridge to chiropractic's long-standing interest in the difference between living and nonliving systems. It does not require treating life as a separate substance or explaining every biological event as purposeful.

## Limits

Self-organization does not mean self-sufficiency. Organisms depend on physical structure, energy, other organisms, and their environments. Their responses are constrained and can fail. A response can preserve short-term viability while reducing longer-term flexibility.
