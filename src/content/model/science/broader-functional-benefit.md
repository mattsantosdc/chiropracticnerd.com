## Why this claim is present

S-006 proposes a downstream causal possibility: some neuromotor changes produced by chiropractic may contribute to functional gains beyond the immediate neuromotor response. Improved neuromotor function can itself be valuable under P-002; this entry asks whether additional, broader gains occur. “Net-positive” requires judging meaningful gains together with relevant harms, burdens, and tradeoffs, using P-002's criterion. A change in a test is not, by itself, broader health, wellness, or functional benefit.

The Model proposes nervous-system-mediated change as the principal route by which chiropractic inputs may influence broader function. This is a chiropractic-specific empirical commitment, stronger than S-001's general integrative role. It is neither established by that role nor a claim that every effect must be exclusively neural. Particular pathways, the role of neuromotor change as a mediator or indicator, and the proposed primacy of neural mediation all remain revisable.

The S-005 dependency now records that this hypothesis specifically concerns chiropractic-produced neuromotor changes. It does not fill the empirical bridge. Studies must establish the input's causal contribution to the neuromotor change, the relationship of that change to a prespecified broader outcome, and relevant population, state, dose, and timescale overlap. Association alone cannot identify mediation.

## Boundary

“Some” and “may” preserve an unresolved possibility, not an established effect, a frequency estimate, or a universal promise. Immediate change does not establish causation, durability, mechanism, or broader benefit. Evidence that an input changes a neuromotor measure must not stand in for evidence of transfer.

If this pathway fails, the proposed explanation must be revised; failure does not establish the impossibility of every chiropractic effect by another route. Conversely, a broader outcome observed without evidence for neural mediation would not validate this mechanism. Specific testable predictions and evidence remain necessary.
