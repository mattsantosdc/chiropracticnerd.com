## Why this claim is present

S-006 proposes a downstream causal possibility: some neuromotor changes produced by chiropractic may contribute to functional gains beyond the immediate neuromotor response. Improved neuromotor function can itself be valuable under P-002; this entry asks whether additional, broader gains occur. “Net-positive” requires judging meaningful gains together with relevant harms, burdens, and tradeoffs, using P-002's criterion. A change in a test is not, by itself, broader health, wellness, or functional benefit.

Studies must establish the input's causal contribution to the neuromotor change, the contribution of that change to a prespecified broader outcome, and relevant population, state, dose, and timescale overlap. The value of any broader gain must be assessed over an appropriate duration and weighed against harms, burdens, and tradeoffs. A neuromotor measure may be an indicator rather than a mediator of change; association alone cannot establish its causal role.

S-007 separately proposes neural mediation as the principal route to broader chiropractic effects. This entry concerns net-positive benefit; it does not require that neural mediation be the principal pathway. Neither hypothesis establishes the other.

## Current evidence

This entry has not yet documented evidence establishing the proposed broader beneficial transfer. A neuromotor response alone does not establish that transfer, and S-005's intended use of an input supplies no evidence of broader benefit.

## Boundary

“Some” and “may” preserve an unresolved possibility, not an established effect, a frequency estimate, or a universal promise. Immediate change does not establish causation, durability, mechanism, or broader benefit. Evidence that an input changes a neuromotor measure must not stand in for evidence of transfer.

Failure of a specified transfer hypothesis requires revising that hypothesis; it does not establish the impossibility of every chiropractic effect by another route. Specific testable predictions and evidence remain necessary.
