## Inferential route

M-005 supplies the legitimate aim of net-positive functional improvement. M-006 makes that rationale available without requiring symptoms or pathology. M-009 proposes: “Some motor strategies that limit functional options in a given context can be modified to improve function overall.” Its explanation names a candidate pattern a neuromotor constraint and keeps that pattern distinct from the opportunity for beneficial change. M-011 proposes: “Some chiropractic inputs produce beneficial changes in neuromotor function by perturbing established motor strategies.” This asserts an empirical effect, separately from confidence in its truth.

The practical reasoning scheme is conditional: when pursuing a legitimate aim through an uncertain intervention, identify the relevant present opportunity, assess whether any available input has sufficient expected value, and state the prediction as a revisable working hypothesis. This decision principle is the argument's normative bridge; it is not a causal fact derived from M-009 or M-011. It motivates M-014's assessment strategy and its “what, if any, input” condition, without requiring action.

The scheme also uses the Model's proposed scope of adjustment: its target is a neuromotor constraint that an available input could modify beneficially. A symptom or diagnosis is not itself that target and does not establish the required opportunity. If the opportunity is absent, the proposed adjustment cannot accomplish that neuromotor improvement. Safety and comfort are additional considerations: symptoms or pathology may warrant withholding or modifying an input without supplying its functional indication. This makes the practical target requirement explicit; it is not deduced from M-006's non-necessity claim or offered as evidence that the proposed mechanism is correct.

## Scope and unresolved premises

Even assuming both empirical premises are true, M-009's modifiable opportunities and M-011's beneficial chiropractic effects need not coincide in the same person, state, task, input, or timescale. M-009 does not establish that an opportunity is detectable or responsive to chiropractic. M-011 asserts beneficial effects in some cases; it supplies neither a validated rule for choosing an input nor a guarantee of success in the present case. M-006 permits a rationale without symptoms; it supplies no efficacy evidence in asymptomatic people.

A warranted decision to proceed would additionally require sufficiently reliable observations, a credible match between the proposed opportunity and available input, and a benefit-risk judgment that accounts for the person's goals, preferences, safety, alternatives, and context. Those person-level conditions remain to be established; they are not accepted empirical premises hidden in this argument. An observed neuromotor response would also be insufficient to justify broader benefit.

The distinction between symptoms and adjustment targets does not establish that every symptom or diagnosis is empirically unrelated to neuromotor function. Nor does failure to detect a target establish that none exists. The validity of particular indicators and the proposed mechanism require independent evaluation. An unsuccessful input must remain a possible failure of the assessment, selection, or mechanism; it cannot be excluded from evaluation merely by retrospectively declaring that it was not an adjustment.

The conclusion is therefore a defeasible strategy for inquiry and selection, not proof that the assessment works or that an actionable opportunity exists. No actionable opportunity identified is an admissible result. A candidate constraint specifies what assessment seeks to evaluate; its definition supplies no evidence for detection, beneficial change, or input selection.
