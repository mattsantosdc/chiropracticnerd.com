## Learning from reassessment

Repeating the relevant assessment after a chiropractic input turns the art into an iterative learning process. The post-application findings may support the working hypothesis, fail to support it, or suggest that the pattern or selected input was misunderstood. They then guide the decision to stop, revise the hypothesis, or consider a different input when justified. Further input is conditional, not the default response to an unchanged finding.

Reassessment findings inform the working hypothesis and selection judgment in the next assessment cycle described in S-014.

Interpret the findings against the specified neuromotor scope, context, baseline or alternative, timescale, and competing functional demands. Ask separately whether the observations support reorganization, improvement, and the input's causal contribution. A different test result can leave any of these uncertain. S-025's success criterion does not require uncertain observations to be labeled either successful or failed. All delivered inputs and their outcomes remain relevant to evaluating the strategy.

## Boundary

Within-cycle reassessment tests the working hypothesis; it does not establish that the input caused the observed change. Natural variability, repeated testing, expectation, measurement error, and other contextual influences remain alternatives. Immediate findings also do not establish the proposed mechanism, durability beyond the evaluated interval, or broader functional benefit; the assessment methods and decision thresholds still require independent evaluation.

An established functional benefit would still not require further inputs or show that the intervention was worth all its costs and risks. A favorable local result can coexist with relevant losses or better alternatives. Reassessment informs a fresh decision, including stopping, rather than creating a requirement to continue.
